## 2. Problem Statement & Systemic Industry Need

### 1. The $3.4 Billion Annual Misconduct Taxpayer Crisis
Over **$3.4 billion** is paid out annually by American municipalities and risk pools to settle police excessive force and wrongful death claims ($12M Breonna Taylor, $27M George Floyd, $19M Elijah McClain). Cities lack predictive early-warning systems to identify repeat offenders before catastrophic liability occurs.

### 2. Aggressive Specialized Tactical Squads & ICE Encroachment
Vulnerable communities, immigrant families, and youth face aggressive street stops and door-to-door administrative sweeps by ICE and roving specialized tactical squads. Citizens frequently succumb to coercive entry due to confusing administrative papers (DHS Form I-200/I-205) with valid Judicial Search Warrants signed by Court Judges.

### 3. Coercive Interrogation of Minors on School Grounds
School Resource Officers (SROs) and youth squads frequently subject minors to custodial interrogations without parental presence, violating the Supreme Court youth custody standard established in *J.D.B. v. North Carolina* (564 U.S. 261) and *In re Gault* (387 U.S. 1).

### 4. Siloed "Brady Lists" & The Wandering Officer Loophole
Under *Brady v. Maryland* (373 U.S. 83), prosecutors must disclose officer credibility and misconduct records. However, Brady lists remain siloed in county paper binders, enabling officers terminated for perjury or brutality in one county to be rehired in another (*"wandering officers"*).

### 5. Bodycam Redaction Bottlenecks & Video Alteration Risks
Bodycam and dashcam footage is frequently delayed, selectively withheld, or buried behind costly FOIA barriers. Litigation teams spend hundreds of unbillable hours manually syncing contradictory camera angles and audio feeds.

## 5. Legal & Evidentiary Frameworks

* **Federal Rule of Evidence 1006**: Summaries to prove voluminous recordings as certified trial matrixes.
* **42 U.S.C. § 1983**: Statutory claims for Fourth, Eighth, and Fourteenth Amendment constitutional deprivations.
* **Payton v. New York (445 U.S. 573)**: Prohibition on warrantless entry into private homes without consent.
* **J.D.B. v. North Carolina (564 U.S. 261) & In re Gault (387 U.S. 1)**: Mandatory consideration of minor age in custody interrogations and youth right to counsel.
* **Riley v. California (573 U.S. 373)**: Warrant requirement for searching digital phone contents.
* **Rodriguez v. United States (575 U.S. 348)**: Unconstitutional prolongation of traffic stops beyond investigatory scope.
* **Monell v. Dept. of Social Services (436 U.S. 658)**: Municipal liability for unconstitutional customs and failure to train.
* **Brady v. Maryland (373 U.S. 83) & Giglio v. United States (405 U.S. 150)**: Mandatory disclosure of exculpatory evidence and officer credibility findings.
* **CJIS Compliance**: TLS 1.3 encryption in transit, AES-256 at rest, and strict role-based access control.
