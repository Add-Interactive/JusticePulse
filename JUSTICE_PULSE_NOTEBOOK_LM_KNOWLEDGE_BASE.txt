2. PROBLEM STATEMENT & SYSTEMIC INDUSTRY NEED
================================================================================
American municipal law enforcement and civil rights litigation face four systemic 
points of failure:

1. THE $3.4 BILLION MISCONDUCT TAXPAYER CRISIS
   Over $3.4 billion is paid out annually by American cities and insurance risk 
   pools to settle police misconduct claims (e.g. $12M Breonna Taylor, $27M George 
   Floyd, $19M Elijah McClain). Cities lack early-warning telemetry to identify 
   repeat offender officers before catastrophic liability occurs.

2. THE "BRADY LIST" OPACITY & WANDERING OFFICER PHENOMENON
   Under Brady v. Maryland (373 U.S. 83), prosecutors must disclose exculpatory 
   officer credibility issues. However, Brady lists remain siloed in county binders, 
   allowing officers fired for excessive force or perjury in one jurisdiction to 
   be rehired one county over ("wandering officers").

3. BODYCAM TAMPERING, REDACTION FRICTION & DISCOVERY CHOKEPOINTS
   Bodycam and dashcam footage is frequently delayed, selectively leaked, or 
   heavily redacted behind costly FOIA barriers. Civil rights defense lawyers 
   spend hundreds of hours manually syncing conflicting video angles and acoustic 
   timelines without specialized software.

4. ASYMMETRICAL RESOURCES FOR VICTIMS & TRIAL PRACTITIONERS
   Municipalities deploy taxpayer-funded city attorneys and police union defense 
   reservoirs, while victims and solo civil rights attorneys work on contingency 
   with fragmented tools, making Monell pattern-and-practice discovery cost-prohibitive.

5. LEGAL & EVIDENTIARY FRAMEWORKS
================================================================================
Justice Pulse is built strictly around established American jurisprudence:

• FEDERAL RULE OF EVIDENCE 1006 (Summaries to Prove Content):
  Allows voluminous bodycam video, telemetry logs, and dispatch records to be 
  presented in courtroom trial proceedings as certified summary charts and matrixes.

• 42 U.S.C. § 1983 (Civil Action for Deprivation of Rights):
  Primary statutory vehicle for constitutional civil rights claims under the 
  Fourth, Eighth, and Fourteenth Amendments.

• MONELL V. DEPT. OF SOCIAL SERVICES (436 U.S. 658):
  Establishes municipal liability when unconstitutional actions implement or execute 
  a policy statement, ordinance, regulation, or pervasive informal custom.

• BRADY V. MARYLAND (373 U.S. 83) & GIGLIO V. UNITED STATES (405 U.S. 150):
  Constitutional requirement for prosecution to disclose evidence favorable to the 
  defense, including officer credibility, prior untruthfulness, and discipline.

• CRIMINAL JUSTICE INFORMATION SERVICES (CJIS) COMPLIANCE:
  Encryption in transit (TLS 1.3) and at rest (AES-256) with strict role-based 
  access controls ensuring evidentiary chain of custody.
